Growth strategy

Before deciding where the money goes, we build a simple growth model. Say the target is VND 2 billion a month and the average order is VND 500,000. That means 4,000 orders. How many come from new customers, how many from returning ones, and how much can you afford to pay for each new customer? Once those three numbers exist, splitting the budget gets much easier.

How an engagement works

Five stages. Tracking comes before spend, which is the part many projects get backwards.

  1. Audit. We read your sales data, ad accounts, Google Analytics and Search Console, and talk to whoever handles sales. View access is enough at this stage. You get a short written assessment: which channels make money and where the measurement is off.
  2. Strategy. Targets are set in business terms (revenue, CAC, break-even ROAS), and the budget is split by channel and by month.
  3. Tracking and launch. Pixels, conversion events and UTM tags go in first. We check that ad-reported orders match your order system, then turn campaigns on.
  4. Optimization. Weekly reviews of creative, bids and budget. We avoid constant tinkering: Meta says an ad set typically needs around 50 results in the week after its last significant edit to exit the learning phase.
  5. Reporting and replanning. Reports reconcile against revenue in your own sales system, not only what the ad platforms claim. Periodic incrementality tests show which channels add revenue that would not have happened anyway.

How to choose a digital marketing agency in Ho Chi Minh City

Use these questions on every agency you talk to. They apply to us as well.

  • Who owns the ad accounts? Google Ads, Meta Business, TikTok, your pixels and Analytics should sit under your company, with the agency added as a partner. If you part ways, the history stays with you.
  • Is the fee separate from media spend? You should see the platform invoices directly. Ask whether any markup is added to media costs.
  • How are invoices issued? Foreign companies should confirm early who issues the Vietnamese VAT invoice for fees and for ad spend.
  • Do they ask about your margins? An agency that never asks about gross margin, average order value or repeat rate is planning around channel metrics, not your P&L.
  • How do they measure? Who installs tracking, how reported conversions are reconciled with real revenue, and which attribution model they use.
  • Who does the actual work? Ask for the names of the people running your account, their certifications and how many clients each of them handles.
  • Do they promise rankings? Google states plainly that no one can guarantee a top ranking. A fixed ROAS promise made before anyone has seen your data deserves the same skepticism.
  • What does the contract say? Minimum term, notice period, exit terms, and who owns the creative and content afterward.

FAQ

How do digital marketing agencies charge?

Three models are common. A monthly retainer is easy to budget but is not tied to results. A percentage of ad spend grows with your budget, which gives the agency little reason to suggest cutting a channel that is not working. Performance-based fees (per lead, order or share of revenue) need a precise definition of what counts and which system does the counting. Many contracts combine a base fee with a performance bonus. We quote after the audit, once the scope is clear.

Will the agency own my ad accounts?

It should not. Ad accounts, pixels and Analytics properties belong in your name, with the agency working through partner access. That is how we set things up, so ending the relationship means removing our access and nothing more.

How long before I see results from ads and SEO?

Ads produce data in the first week, but platforms need a few weeks to learn before the numbers settle. SEO is slower. Google's SEO Starter Guide says some changes show up in hours and others take several months, and suggests waiting a few weeks before judging. We do not promise rankings or a fixed ROAS.

What should I prepare for the first meeting?

Monthly revenue for the last 6 to 12 months, gross margin, average order value, an indicative budget and view access to your ad and analytics accounts. With those, the first conversation can be about a plan rather than introductions.

Do I still need someone in-house?

Yes, at least one person. They hold the business targets, approve content, share sales data and make decisions when the agency proposes changes. Without that role, most projects stall waiting for approvals.
